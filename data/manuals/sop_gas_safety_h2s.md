# SOP-03 Gas safety and H2S

## Safety first
Biogas contains methane (flammable) and hydrogen sulfide H2S (toxic). H2S smells like rotten eggs at low levels,
but at high concentrations it paralyses the sense of smell: never trust your nose.

## Personal H2S detector alarm or gas leak suspicion
1. Leave the area immediately, moving upwind. Do not try to find the leak alone.
2. Warn other people and the control room. Activate the gas alarm if not already active.
3. Re-entry only with a self-contained breathing apparatus and a second person outside (buddy system).
4. Exposure limit: 10 ppm for 8 hours. 100 ppm is immediately dangerous to life and health.

## High H2S in biogas (analyser AT_203)
Normal value after desulfurisation is below 500 ppm. The CHP engine limit is 700 ppm.
A steady upward trend over several weeks means the desulfurisation media (iron oxide or activated carbon) is saturated.
Action: schedule media replacement with maintenance and increase air dosing for biological desulfurisation within limits.

## High pressure in gas holder (alarm PAH, PT_204 above 25 mbar)
Check that the CHP engine is running and consuming gas. If the CHP is stopped, start the flare to burn excess gas.
Never vent biogas directly to the atmosphere.
